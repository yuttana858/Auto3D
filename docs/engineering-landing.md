# Agentic engineering entry experience

The public entry screen combines product geometry, structural packaging, agent-assisted workflows and sign-in in one floating grey-and-blue banner on a black background. Layered bevels, a raised lower edge and inset controls provide depth. Desktop places the animation alongside account access; mobile stacks the animation above account access. The compact desktop banner is approximately half the previous height. The screenshot reference is recreated as procedural 3D presentation geometry, with a moving transition between brown kraft paper surfaces and a blue CAD cutaway. It is an illustrative workflow, not a generated manufacturing design or a claim of automatic insert optimization.

`engineeringlanding.js` composes the banner, stage controls and existing account form. `engineeringintro.js` constructs the Auto3D wordmark with SVG outlines, projection guides, centerlines, construction circles and dimension marks, then colorizes it, reveals “Automated Intelligent Packaging System,” holds, then fades the mark. The same presentation clock drives the logo, glass prompt entrance and docking, so pause/replay also freezes or restarts the opening. `engineeringhero.js` owns the presentation scene, clipping, rendering, playback and disposal. `engineeringpack.js` builds and poses the folding structure: opposing large returns each reach halfway across the pack, short locking tongues fold out of relief cuts in the outer lids, and matching cut-through slots receive them in the inner lids. The inner lids sit slightly below the outer lids for insertion clearance. Matching rounded hand holes are cut through the opposing side walls; the pulp cradles leave the full finger openings clear. `engineeringpulp.js` creates exactly two continuous left/right molded-pulp cradles. Each full-length L-shaped sheet joins the front and rear supports with drafted reinforcing ribs, a reinforced top edge and bottom foot supports; finger openings leave continuous upper and lower side webs. The sheet has two offset surfaces and sealed cut edges, rather than filled protective blocks. A morph target forms the initially flat sheet into the fitted side/bottom structure as it approaches the motor. Warm kraft and pulp materials use distinct procedural grain and cellulose-fiber bump textures. `engineeringcamera.js` frames the completed motor at 94% of the rendering window for the opening. It retains the 50% pulp shot and 35% carton-reveal shot, with pullbacks at scene times 3.8–5.2 and 7.2–8.6. From 8.6–15.6, it eases toward the completed carton's silhouette, zooming inward and shifting the focus to the box center as the panels close. The finished box fills 96% of the limiting viewport dimension, with the fit following its gentle rotation and float through cutaway. The closed reference is captured once in assembly coordinates and transformed with the current presentation pose. Open flaps may crop without driving an extra pullback. The shared timeline handles pause, stage selection, replay, reduced motion and resizing. `engineeringlanding.css` is scoped to the entry screen. Production bundles all Three.js dependencies locally; no CDN, generated image or external model is needed.

`engineeringentrance.js` runs a quick, overlapping 1.4-second card entrance: the beveled frame flashes, navigation and introductory text descend, “Engineering intelligence.” types in, and the Welcome/sign-in area descends and flashes into place while the logo animation starts. The heading reserves its final dimensions to avoid layout shifts. The entrance uses the presentation clock, pauses with playback, runs on initial load and explicit Replay, and stays settled across automatic loops. Reduced motion, unavailable WebGL or focusing a login control immediately completes the entrance. It adds no time or height to the existing banner.

The sign-in form uses the existing AuthService and account request services. The current saved-session check runs on load; a returning visitor stays on the banner until Continue to workspace. The header Sign in action focuses the form and Check saved sign-in retries restoration. The existing automated-browser path is preserved. Direct form submission starts the existing sign-in lifecycle. The existing cookie/session policy and approved-account model are unchanged. Recovery links go directly to the reset form. Request submissions and credential entry are interactive. Login fields use browser autocomplete; busy state prevents duplicate submissions and password visibility resets after attempts.

The presentation-only prompt has translucent glass surfaces, reflective edge highlights and backdrop blur. It first appears centered on an otherwise empty rendering area. The agent greeting reads “Hi Glen, what would you like to create today?” Glen’s “Create 3D model US-54783.” request then types in before the panel shrinks into the lower-left corner. Product geometry stays hidden until docking and the agent reply finish; blue outlines draw progressively before the metal surfaces resolve. Its 32-pixel prompt row and compact reply/progress area replace the previous full-width interactive composer. The three scripted requests generate `US-54783`, use the MP tool for side/bottom molded-pulp protection, and assemble `EC-US54783-110926`. The first request types character by character; the next two reveal whole words to suggest speech-to-text while a blue mic pulses. Agent replies use a blue backlight and waveform. The mic and send glyphs are decorative, with no keyboard focus, click handlers or editable content. No speech synthesis, speech recognition, microphone or AI/CAD request is invoked.

The shared 35-second sequence adds a nine-second opening while preserving the previous processing pace. It holds geometry during requests and replies, then runs processing and the matching 3D operation. The final cutaway remains visible before the loop repeats. Pause, replay and stage selection control the same timeline; pausing or hiding the presentation also freezes the cursor, mic, waveform and backlight. The camera keeps the motor centered for the initial pullbacks, then zooms in and centers the closed box as it folds; unfolded panels may crop beyond the window. The glass prompt stays compact in the lower-left corner, with its execution row hidden during completed/review states to expose more of the structure.

The workspace stays inert until authentication and account preferences are ready. Signing out locks it immediately. The preview renderer is disposed when entering the workspace and recreated after logout; reduced motion initially shows a still cutaway. Automatic playback pauses when the visual leaves the viewport or the document is hidden. An explicit Play action can run the demo with reduced motion enabled.

Public commands share the same entry controller used by the UI:

- `auth.open`: focuses sign-in while locked, or opens the account dialog inside the workspace; does not authenticate.
- `auth.continue`: preserves the existing explicit Continue action for a restored account.
- `landing.open`: focuses the overview within the banner while signed out.
- `landing.demo.stage`: pauses at stage 0–3.
- `landing.demo.playback`: play, pause or restart.
- `landing.inspect`: reads view, scripted conversation, entrance, current camera fit and playback state without account data; marks the overlay presentation-only.
- `landing.prompt.submit`: retained for compatibility with existing macros; returns `accepted: false` and `presentationOnly: true` without changing playback.
- `landing.prompt.voice`: retained for compatibility; returns `enabled: false` and `presentationOnly: true` without accessing audio or media.

These presentation commands are available while signed out. Existing model commands remain protected. The generated browser and MCP catalogs include the new commands.
